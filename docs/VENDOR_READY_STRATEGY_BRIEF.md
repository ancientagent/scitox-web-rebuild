This brief keeps vendor outreach as the primary goal for the SciTOX rebuild. It is not final website copy, not a legal review, and not evidence that any vendor, distributor, retailer, marketplace, or platform will accept SciTOX.

## Strategy Goal

Make SciTOX credible enough for controlled vendor, wholesale, smoke shop, head shop, distributor, online retailer, and retailer outreach.

The rebuilt site should make the business look organized, serious, supportable, and review-ready before anyone sends outreach. The public customer site matters because buyers and reviewers will likely inspect it before deciding whether to engage.

## What Vendors Will Care About

Vendors, wholesalers, distributors, and retailers will likely evaluate:

- Product clarity: what the product is, what variants exist, and how the line is organized.
- Buyer fit: whether the product fits their retail environment, customers, and risk tolerance.
- Claim restraint: whether the brand avoids risky, unsupported, or platform-triggering claims.
- Product documentation: labels, ingredients, images, SKU data, UPC/GTIN, case packs, wholesale terms, and policies.
- Margin potential: wholesale price, MSRP, COGS context, shipping burden, returns, and support load.
- Fulfillment reliability: whether SciTOX can ship vendor orders consistently.
- Brand seriousness: whether the public site feels stable, professional, and trustworthy.
- Support process: whether buyers and customers can get clear help without overloading the vendor.
- Policy clarity: shipping, returns, damaged goods, privacy, support, and terms.
- Platform risk: whether the public brand language could create issues for payment, ad, marketplace, or vendor review.
- Owner readiness: whether the business can support wholesale without the owner becoming the bottleneck.

## Why The Current Site Creates Risk

The current site may already convert high-intent customers, but the vendor path has a different standard. A vendor or reviewer is not only asking whether customers buy; they are asking whether carrying or partnering with the brand creates risk.

Current-site risk areas identified in the prior discussion include:

- Aggressive drug-test/pass-result framing.
- Guarantee and outcome language that may need proof and policy alignment.
- Success-rate or superiority claims that require substantiation.
- Scientific/proven language that may need evidence.
- Substance-specific positioning that can trigger platform or buyer concern.
- Competitor attack language, which can make the brand look unstable.
- AI-generated proof or summary language that should not be treated as substantiation.
- Privacy/security language that may overpromise if the system does not technically support it.
- Unrelated FAQ or product claims that broaden risk.
- Refund, guarantee, policy, or support contradictions that can deter buyers.
- A public site experience that may feel cluttered or too emergency/direct-response oriented for vendor review.

The rebuild should document these issues before rewriting them. Anything claim-sensitive must remain `[REVIEW REQUIRED]`.

## What The Rebuilt Site Must Communicate

The rebuilt site must communicate that SciTOX is:

- A serious specialty product business.
- Organized enough for vendor review.
- Clear about its product line and support model.
- Cautious with claims.
- Prepared to provide product and wholesale documentation.
- Direct and supportive for customers without panic-sales tactics.
- Privacy-aware without unverified privacy/security promises.
- Ready for structured inquiry, not blind outreach.

The rebuilt site should separate:

- Public customer layer: private guidance, calm support, product routing, policies, and restrained product education.
- Vendor/wholesale layer: buyer-facing product data, inquiry path, documentation checklist, wholesale-readiness notes, and restrained claims.

Create or prepare these assets before outreach:

- Vendor-ready strategy brief: this document.
- Product line sheet template.
- Product data sheet for each SKU.
- Wholesale inquiry page.
- Wholesale inquiry form connected to a spreadsheet or CRM.
- Vendor documentation checklist.
- Buyer tracker spreadsheet.
- Claims cleanup list.
- Claim-safe product description placeholders.
- Product image set or image requirements list.
- Product label and packaging file archive.
- Policy summary for shipping, returns, damaged goods, and support.
- Owner-data request list.
- Vendor outreach draft templates for later owner approval.

Do not create final outreach or send vendor messages until the owner approves the data, claims, and target list.

## Wholesale Inquiry Page Requirements

The wholesale inquiry page should be professional, restrained, and buyer-ready. It should not feel like the warmer direct customer journey.

Required page elements:

- Clear page purpose: partner, retailer, distributor, or wholesale inquiry.
- Short restrained brand/product overview with `[REVIEW REQUIRED]` where claims are involved.
- Product line overview with placeholders where owner data is missing.
- Buyer qualification language without gatekeeping theatrics.
- Documentation checklist section.
- Inquiry process: submit inquiry, review, response, documentation exchange, next step.
- Contact/inquiry form.
- Notes that pricing, terms, and product documentation are provided after review, if that is the owner-approved workflow.
- No claims of vendor approval, marketplace eligibility, Amazon acceptance, or platform safety.

Recommended inquiry form fields:

- Contact name.
- Business name.
- Business type: retailer, smoke shop, head shop, distributor, online retailer, wholesaler, other.
- Website or business URL.
- Email.
- Phone.
- Business location.
- Estimated channel or intended use.
- Product interest.
- Current wholesale/vendor account status, if relevant.
- Message.
- Consent to be contacted.

Avoid collecting sensitive customer-style intake details on the vendor inquiry page.

## Buyer Concerns To Address

The strategy should anticipate and address these concerns:

- Will this product create payment, ad, marketplace, or platform risk?
- Are the product claims restrained and supportable?
- Does the brand look serious enough to carry?
- Can SciTOX fulfill wholesale or distributor orders reliably?
- Are margins, MSRP, MOQ, and case packs workable?
- Are labels, UPCs, images, ingredients, and documentation available?
- Are refund, shipping, damaged-goods, and support terms clear?
- Will customer questions create burden for the vendor?
- Is the public site likely to scare buyers, reviewers, or payment partners?
- Is the owner prepared to respond consistently after interest comes in?

The rebuilt site does not need to answer every buyer concern with final data in V1, but it must not hide gaps. Missing facts should be marked `[OWNER DATA NEEDED]`.

## What Requires Review

These items require review before they are used in public, vendor-facing, or outreach materials:

- Drug-test, pass-result, or outcome claims.
- Guarantee language.
- Refund language tied to results.
- Success-rate claims.
- "Most effective," "#1," "proven," "scientifically tested," or superiority language.
- Substance-specific positioning.
- Mechanism-of-action explanations.
- Customer reviews, testimonials, result screenshots, or proof sections.
- Competitor comparisons.
- Safety, health, ingredient, or side-effect statements.
- Privacy, confidentiality, encryption, or AI guidance language.
- Any wording that could sound like legal or medical advice.
- Amazon, vendor, marketplace, payment, or advertising eligibility statements.
- Any policy language affecting returns, disputes, privacy, data retention, or customer support.

Use `[REVIEW REQUIRED]` until review is complete.

## Recommended Vendor-Ready Sequence

1. Finish public-site extraction and claims inventory.
2. Mark risky claims, missing data, and vendor-readiness blockers.
3. Build the clean public customer site structure.
4. Build the separate vendor/wholesale layer structure.
5. Complete product data templates with owner-supplied fields.
6. Prepare the vendor documentation checklist and line sheet.
7. Review claims, privacy, policies, and inquiry flow.
8. Build buyer tracker and outreach drafts.
9. Get owner approval before any vendor outreach.

## Guardrails

- Do not contact vendors yet.
- Do not claim vendor acceptance.
- Do not claim Amazon eligibility.
- Do not invent wholesale economics.
- Do not use unreviewed claims in vendor materials.
- Do not mix the direct customer emotional flow into the vendor-facing page.
- Do not make the vendor-facing layer feel like a panic-response sales page.
- Do not hide missing owner data.
